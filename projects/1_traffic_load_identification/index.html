Research · Theme 01

Traffic Load Identification

Who is on the bridge, where, and how heavy? Combining weigh-in-motion sensors with machine vision to map the traffic load on a whole bridge.

Traffic is the main everyday load on most bridges. To judge whether a bridge is safe, engineers need to know how many vehicles are on it, where they are, and how heavy they are, at every moment. Weigh-in-motion (WIM) sensors can weigh vehicles, but only at one point on the road. Cameras can follow vehicles along the whole deck, but cannot weigh them.

This research combines the two. Vehicles are weighed by WIM sensors and then tracked across the bridge with cameras and deep-learning object detection. The result is the traffic load on the whole bridge, over time.

Main contributions

  • Fusing WIM and multi-camera vision to identify moving loads on a bridge (Dan et al., 2019).
  • Full-bridge load monitoring with YOLO-v3: an accurate and robust method to monitor how traffic load is distributed over the whole bridge (Ge et al., 2020).
  • Real-time safety checks: using the identified positions of moving loads to monitor and evaluate the overturning risk of single-column-pier box-girder bridges (Ge et al., 2020).
  • Traffic load simulation that combines machine vision and WIM monitoring (Ge et al., 2022).
  • Long-term monitoring of long-span bridges with an improved system for full-bridge traffic load distribution (Ge et al., 2023).
  • A benchmark dataset for vision-based traffic load monitoring on a cable-stayed bridge (Ge et al., 2024).

The traffic load data from this work also drives the bridge digital twins described in Digital Twins and Physics-Informed Learning.
